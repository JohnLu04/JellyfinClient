# JellyfinClient (iPhone)

Native SwiftUI client for [Jellyfin](https://jellyfin.org) servers (10.9+). iOS 18+, no third-party dependencies.

## Features

- **Login** – server address (`192.168.1.10:8096`, `https://…`, pasted web URL), username/password; session token stored in the Keychain.
- **Home** – full-width hero carousel (artwork, title logo, genres, age rating, "Play S3 · E2", favorite heart), Continue Watching and Next Up with series artwork, Recently Added per library, libraries.
- **Title pages** (movies, series, episodes) – artwork header with a play button for the trailer, which starts only on a tap and pauses on a tap; its sound comes on together with the picture, once YouTube's title and "More videos" overlay has faded out (German trailers preferred; YouTube via embedded player or local trailer files), Play/Resume and Download, description, cast, Favorite, Share (IMDb link), audio & quality options, Download Season, Episodes / More Like This tabs. **More Like This** comes from Seerr (TMDB recommendations, topped up with similar titles; an episode uses its series): titles in the library (green dot) open their page here, the rest open Seerr to request them. Without Seerr, Jellyfin's own suggestions are shown.
- **Tabs** – Home, Favorites (favorite movies, series and episodes), Library and Request; Downloads and Settings open from buttons at the top right.
- **Netflix Top 10 on Home** – this week's Top 10 movies above the first movie library's "Recently Added" row and Top 10 series above the TV one (same list as in Request). Titles in your library show their poster with a green dot and open their page; the rest open Seerr to request them.
- **Netflix-style categories on Home** – below the "Recently Added" rows: 26 rows like Audience Favorites, US Series, True Stories, Anime, Teen Series, Action Movies, Series That Still Impress 10 Years Later, Hidden Gems, Cult Classics from the 90s, Miniseries for One Weekend … from Seerr's discover endpoint (TMDB genres, keywords, original language, dates, votes, runtime). They come in a new random order on every app launch and load as they scroll into view. Titles on the server have a green dot and open their page; the rest open Seerr. Without Seerr the library's biggest genres are shown instead. The Libraries row is gone (there's a Library tab).
- **Holiday & event rows** – while a window is open they come first among the categories (not shuffled) with an "Until …" line, then disappear by themselves: New Year's Eve (27 Dec–1 Jan), Valentine's Day (7–14 Feb), Carnival (Weiberfastnacht–Ash Wednesday), Easter (Holy Week–Easter Monday, computed per year), May the 4th (1–4 May), Pride Month (June), Summer (July–August), German history (1–3 Oct, 7–9 Nov), Halloween (15–31 Oct), Christmas (25 Nov–26 Dec, more rows from 1 Dec).
- **Search** – separate magnifying-glass button at the bottom right (iOS 27 `prominent` tab role, `search` role before). Finds movies and series in your libraries and, with Seerr connected, suggests matching titles you don't have yet (deduplicated by TMDB id / title + year) to request.
- **Browsing** – libraries (movies, shows, folders), posters with paging, watched/progress badges.
- **Request (Seerr)** – the Seerr integration from the Servarr app: Movies/Series filter, requests card (pending count; approve/decline/delete, new request search), networks (Netflix opens with Netflix's weekly Top 10 for Germany), Trending / Popular / Upcoming / Top Rated rows with "See All" lists (sort, genre, hide available), search, and title pages with IMDb/Rotten Tomatoes/TMDB ratings, streaming availability (JustWatch), seasons and episodes, and requests with season, server and quality-profile choice. Connect it under Settings → Seerr (address + API key, stored in the Keychain).
- **Two players, switchable any time in Settings:**
  - **mpv** (default, via [MPVKit](https://github.com/mpvkit/MPVKit), LGPL) – plays almost every format directly (MKV, HEVC, AV1, DTS, PGS …). In-player menus for **all audio tracks and subtitles**: embedded tracks switch instantly, external/text subtitles are loaded from the server, and while transcoding a switch fetches a new stream at the current position.
  - **Apple player** (AVPlayer) – PiP and AirPlay; anything the iPhone can't play natively is transcoded by the server.
- **Buffer** – Settings → Buffer: how many seconds are loaded ahead, 10 s to 10 min (mpv `cache-secs`/`demuxer-readahead-secs` within a RAM-based memory ceiling; `preferredForwardBufferDuration` for the Apple player).
- **Video details in mpv** – an info button in the player opens a live panel: play method, bitrate limit, container, file size, average/current bitrate, buffered seconds and size, download speed, codecs, resolution, frame rate, hardware decoding, dropped frames, audio channels and sample rate.
- **Automatic quality** – Settings / Playback Options → Quality: Original, Automatic or a fixed limit. Automatic follows the connection without reloading: the server transcodes a ladder (0.7 / 1.5 / 2.5 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 12 / 20 / 40 Mbit/s, never above the original) as Jellyfin HLS rungs cut at the same 6-second points; a local server on 127.0.0.1 (`AdaptiveStream`) gives mpv one continuous playlist and picks each segment's rung from the measured rate (margin 30 % with little buffer, none with 30 s+), how long the current rung's segments take compared to their playing time, and mpv's buffered seconds – down at once when the buffer runs low or a segment takes too long (abandoned and fetched two rungs lower); with a comfortable buffer it tries the next rung when the fetch time predicts it still arrives in under 85 % of its playing time (small segments are measured too slow over Wi-Fi), one rung at a time. Apple's player gets the rungs as a master playlist and is steered with `preferredPeakBitRate` by the same rules (`NativeABRSteering`). The video details show the current quality, measured connection and number of changes.
- **Audio & subtitle choice** on every title page (server defaults pre-selected), used by both players.
- The server decides via `PlaybackInfo` (with a device profile per player): direct play when possible, otherwise HLS remux/transcode. Start/progress/stop (incl. selected tracks) are reported so resume positions sync.
- **Transcoding in 0.5 Mbit/s steps** – "Original" or a limit from 0.5 to 120 Mbit/s (stepper + slider). Default in Settings, overridable per title. Resolution is capped automatically at low bitrates.
- **Downloads** – original file or server-transcoded H.264/AAC at a chosen bitrate (0.5 steps), with progress, speed, poster, size estimate and offline playback. Built like Streamyfin's native downloader: transfers run in a background `URLSession` (they keep going when you leave the app, lock the screen or iOS ends the app, and iOS relaunches the app to file them), one at a time from a persisted queue, with automatic retries. **Interruptions don't start over:** originals continue from URLSession's resume data (also after the app was swiped away – the cancelled transfer's data is picked up on the next launch); transcodes are fetched as 24-second fMP4 HLS segments (`main.m3u8`, no stream copy, two segments in flight – Jellyfin restarts a transcode when a request is more than 24 s / segment length ahead) and appended in order to one file, so only the segment in flight is lost. Jellyfin can't resume a progressive transcode (`Accept-Ranges: none`). **Audio tracks & subtitles:** a transcoded file can only carry one audio track (Jellyfin's transcode maps a single stream, and its audio-only endpoint always takes the default track), so the other audio tracks are fetched afterwards as tiny-picture transcodes (160×90, 48 kbit/s picture) with the chosen `audioStreamIndex` – in 24-second fMP4 HLS segments like the video, so a dropped connection only costs the segment in flight and the track continues where it stopped (also after pausing or closing the app); the assembled file is `<id>.extraN.mp4`. While a track loads it has its own progress bar like the video's – in the download list ("Audio track 1 of 2 · German", size, speed), on the title page, in the episode list and on the Lock Screen ("Loading audio track", size, speed, percent); its size is estimated from the bitrate at first and from the segments that are there once two have arrived. Their segment lists are fetched right after the video's, while the app is surely running, so when the video finishes in the background or with the iPhone locked – where a plain request can't run – the tracks go straight on in iOS' download service (a list that couldn't be fetched ahead is requested when the track is due, with a little background time from iOS). The keep-alive's progress covers the whole download, video and audio tracks weighted by their estimated size and never going backwards, since iOS ends a continued-processing task whose progress stops moving. A dropped or missing connection (connection lost, offline, timeout, host unreachable) is retried for much longer than server errors (up to 120 attempts, at most a minute apart, about two hours), and an audio segment that gets through resets the count, so a flaky connection doesn't make a track give up. Text subtitles as `.srt`/`.ass` files; originals get their external subtitles. Subtitles are small, so they're fetched right at the start next to the video, while the app is surely running (the server may first have to extract them from the file, so they get up to 10 minutes); left for the end they'd often be due in the background with the iPhone locked, where iOS may postpone new transfers for hours. One that couldn't be fetched ahead is loaded after the audio tracks with the usual retries (dropped connections up to 120 times, server errors six times before it's skipped), with its own progress bar ("Subtitle 1 of 2 · English", size, speed; "Loading subtitle" on the Lock Screen), and the keep-alive's progress counts it too, so it doesn't reach the end before the subtitles are there. mpv adds all of them as selectable tracks offline. Toggles in the Download sheet. On iOS 26 a continued-processing task keeps the app running in the background, so nothing pauses: iOS postpones transfers that a background session *starts* while the app is in the background (each segment, extra file and queued episode is a new transfer), so while the app is kept running those start in an ordinary in-app session instead. When iOS ends the keep-alive, they are handed back to the background session (originals with their resume data). Debug builds can simulate the keep-alive in the simulator, which has no BGTaskScheduler, with `JF_DEBUG_KEEPALIVE=1`: it runs until the normal background time (about 30 s) is up. **Lock Screen progress:** a Live Activity (the `DownloadActivity` widget extension) shows the title, poster, progress bar, size, speed and waiting titles on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island; one activity follows the whole queue (iOS only lets an app start one while it is in the foreground) and shows how the last title ended for five minutes. The poster travels as a tiny JPEG inside the activity's data (4 KB limit), so no App Group is needed. A notification tells you when a download finishes or fails. If iOS only grants read access to a finished file, it is copied or read in chunks. **Pause and resume:** any download can be paused (tap it, swipe, the title page's download button, or Pause All) and resumed whenever you like, also after closing the app; the transfers in flight are cancelled with their resume data, so it continues where it stopped, and the next download in the queue starts meanwhile. **Offline watch progress:** a download continues where it was stopped (the download's own position; while it was never played offline, the server's), past 90 % it counts as watched. The position is kept with the download and sent to the server (`/UserItems/{id}/UserData`) right away or, when offline, as soon as the server is reachable; streaming a downloaded title updates the download's position too. The download list shows how far each one was watched. **Progress ring:** once a download has started, its button in the episode list and on the title page becomes a thin ring that fills up in red, clockwise from the top (grey with a pause sign while paused, grey while waiting in the queue); tapping it pauses or resumes. **Series folders:** downloaded episodes aren't listed one by one but in a folder per series (with the series poster, number of seasons and episodes, size, and the episode loading right now with its progress bar), and inside it a folder per season – or, with only one season, straight the episodes, numbered. Movies stay in the list as before. Swiping a folder deletes all its episodes after asking. Each download remembers its series, season and episode number; downloads from before are filled in from the server (and grouped by the "Series · S1 · E2" line meanwhile, also offline). Episodes show their season's poster (or the series') in the download list. The download list has a **Download Log** (top left) with every step and error code, to copy and share.
- **Picture in Picture** – Apple's player has it built in (also automatically when you swipe home during playback). mpv draws with Vulkan/Metal, which Picture in Picture can't show and which isn't allowed in the background, so its PiP button hands the video to an AVPlayer at the same position (the downloaded file, or a stream the server prepares for Apple's player) and hands it back to mpv at the new position when you return; closing the PiP window ends playback. While PiP runs, the player screen closes so you can use the app.
- **Quality while playing (mpv)** – the button above the seek bar shows the current streaming quality; picking Original, Automatic or a fixed limit asks the server for a new stream and continues at the same spot. It's also the way out when a stream fails. (Apple's player has no room for custom controls on iPhone.)
- **Screen stays on** while a video plays (also with Low Power Mode's short Auto-Lock); paused, closed or in Picture in Picture it may sleep as usual.
- **Lock Screen / Control Center player** – title, series and episode, poster, progress, play/pause, ±10 s and scrubbing for both players (`NowPlayingController`); mpv keeps playing the sound in the background; the video track stays selected (only the output is switched off), so the buffer survives the screen going off and playback continues seamlessly when it comes back. mpv runs with `audio-exclusive=yes`: otherwise its iOS audio output sets the audio session to mix with others, and a mixing app can't be the Now Playing app.

## Run

```bash
open JellyfinClient.xcodeproj
```

Pick an iPhone simulator or your device and press ⌘R. For a real device, select your team under *Signing & Capabilities* and change the bundle ID (`com.example.JellyfinClient`).

Server-side permissions needed per user: *Allow video playback that requires transcoding* (transcoded playback/downloads) and *Allow media downloading* (original downloads).

## Releases (IPA)

Unsigned IPAs for sideloading (AltStore, SideStore, Sideloadly) are attached to [GitHub releases](../../releases).

- **New release:** `git tag v0.2.0 && git push origin v0.2.0` – the *Build IPA* workflow builds the IPA (version taken from the tag) and creates the release.
- **Test build:** Actions → *Build IPA* → *Run workflow*; the IPA is kept as a workflow artifact for 14 days.
- **Locally:** `scripts/build-ipa.sh [version] [build-number]` → `build/ipa/`.

## Layout

| Folder | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `App/` | App entry, `AppModel` (session), root/tab views |
| `Core/` | `JellyfinAPI` (REST client), models, device profile, bitrate helpers, Keychain storage |
| `Playback/` | `PlaybackSession` (PlaybackInfo → stream URL), `PlaybackCoordinator` (presentation, reporting), `NativePlayerEngine` (AVPlayer) |
| `Playback/MPV/` | `MPVCore` (libmpv wrapper, Metal/MoltenVK rendering), `MPVPlayerController` (state + track switching), `MPVPlayerView` (controls) |
| `Downloads/` | `DownloadManager` – background URLSession, queue, HLS segments, retries, index in `Documents/Downloads`; `DownloadModels` – records, parts, HLS segment list; `DownloadKeepAlive` – iOS 26 continued-processing task; `DownloadActivityController` – Lock Screen Live Activity; `DownloadDiagnostics` – download log |
| `DownloadActivity/` | Widget extension drawing the download Live Activity (Lock Screen, Dynamic Island) |
| `Shared/` | `DownloadActivityAttributes` – the Live Activity's data, compiled into the app and the extension |
| `Views/` | SwiftUI screens and shared components |

New Swift files dropped into `JellyfinClient/` are picked up by Xcode automatically (folder-synchronized group).

## Known limitations (next steps)

- Swiping the app away in the app switcher (force quit) stops downloads until you open the app again; iOS doesn't let any app continue after that. They then continue where they stopped. While the app is in the background **without** the iOS 26 keep-alive (older iOS, or iOS ended it), iOS may wait a while before starting the next segments of a transcode; with the keep-alive or the app open they run at full speed.
- mpv has no Picture in Picture / AirPlay (use the Apple player for that); music, live TV and photo libraries are hidden.
- The first build downloads MPVKit's prebuilt frameworks (~1 GB, cached afterwards).
- Offline watch progress isn't synced back to the server.
- Debug builds accept `JF_DEBUG_SERVER`, `JF_DEBUG_USER`, `JF_DEBUG_PASSWORD` launch environment variables to prefill the login form, `JF_DEBUG_SEERR` / `JF_DEBUG_SEERR_KEY` for the Seerr form and `JF_DEBUG_SEARCH` for the search field, `JF_DEBUG_DATE` (e.g. `2026-12-20`) to try the holiday rows, `JF_DEBUG_PLAY_DOWNLOAD` (a download's item id) to play it right after launch and `JF_DEBUG_DOWNLOAD` (e.g. `e1:0:8:15`: download e1 as original, or at that many Mbit/s, pause at 8 s, resume at 15 s), `JF_DEBUG_PLAY_ITEM` (an item id) to stream it right after launch, `JF_DEBUG_SHOW_QUALITY` to open mpv's quality list, `JF_DEBUG_PAUSE` to pause at 8 s and resume at 12 s, `JF_DEBUG_SKIPS` (e.g. `6:10,10:600,20:q8,30:x`, seconds after start : seconds to skip, `q` and a quality in Mbit/s to switch to, or `x` to close the player) to seek and print the player position every second (used for automated simulator tests; not compiled into Release).
